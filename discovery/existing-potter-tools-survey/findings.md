# Existing potter tools: survey findings

**Kind of hop: a survey** of things outside the project (pottery-tracking apps, glaze databases and calculators, notebooks, spreadsheets and templates). It writes findings, not a design space, and it makes no decisions. Scope is tools for an individual potter. Shared studio galleries and studio-management products are out of scope for this version; where a tool also has a community feed, that part is noted in one line and not surveyed.

**How well each claim is sourced.** Pages opened and read in full are listed first under [Sources](#sources): the PotteryDave home page and blog post, the ClayLab, Bisque-It and Pottery Log (Kenney) App Store pages, the Claywell home and guides pages, The Potter's Mud Room page and the PotteryNotes home page. Every other page cited was seen only as a search-result excerpt, so a claim that rests on one is marked "(excerpt)" where it is made, in the tables, or is listed as excerpt-only under Sources. Excerpts are short and may leave out qualifications that the full page states. Store pages and vendor sites are the vendors' own words about themselves; the only evidence of what potters dislike is user reviews and two Ceramic Arts Daily forum threads, which are anecdotes, not a sample.

## Answers to the three charter questions

1. **Which tools, and what does each record?** Four families are in use. (a) Dedicated apps that follow a piece through stages: PotteryNotes, Pottery Log (two unrelated apps share the name), ClayLab, Dasti, PotteryDave, Claywell, The Potter's Mud Room, Bisque-It, Ceramispace and others. They share a core of photos, stage or status, clay body, glazes, firing details, dimensions and notes. (b) Glaze databases and calculators (Glazy, Digitalfire Insight and Insight-live), which record *recipes and tests*, not individual pots. (c) Paper notebooks, often with phone photos. (d) General tools bent to the job: OneNote, Apple Notes, Airtable, Google Sheets, Notion and Etsy printable templates. See [Tool-by-tool survey](#tool-by-tool-survey).
2. **How is a finished photo kept with its glaze combination, and what is disliked?** Mostly by putting both on one piece record. A few tools make a single image or sheet that carries both (PotteryDave's datasheet card, Glazy's recipe card, ClayLab's finished-piece image, Claywell's image or PDF sheet), and two keep a glaze combination as a named, reusable entry (The Potter's Mud Room, Bisque-It). Paper users photograph the page beside the pot. The dislikes that recur are: only one glaze per surface (so layered combinations go into free-text notes), unrecorded layer order, glaze names typed inconsistently so search misses pots, and losing photos or having no way to get records out. See [How each pairs a finished photo with its glaze](#how-each-pairs-a-finished-photo-with-its-glaze) and [What potters dislike](#what-potters-dislike).
3. **Which export formats open without the app?** Documented ones: JSON backup, CSV spreadsheet, and image or PDF sheets (Claywell, an iPhone beta); CSV for clay bodies, glazes, firing logs, pieces and sales (The Potter's Mud Room, with pieces and sales on paid tiers); PDF (PotteryNotes, excerpt); CSV and YAML (Glazy), a recipe-card image (Glazy), XML recipe files (Insight); and images (ClayLab finished pieces, Ceramispace recipes). The help text for five apps names no format: ClayLab Pro's backup export, PotteryDave's "Download everything", Bisque-It's export of project data and pictures, Pottery Log's file export, and Dasti's "export anytime". Whether those open without the app is not established, and no actual export file from any app was examined. ClayLab's promised CSV export is not in the version history on its store page, which runs to version 2.10.1. See [Export formats and whether they open without the app](#export-formats-and-whether-they-open-without-the-app).

## How the project's own facts bear on this

From the maintainer (Project Memory): they do not track pots today, so there is no old data to import; they want it installable on the home screen; they expect two or three friends to try it. The vision's goal is to follow each pot from lump of clay to finished piece and to let studio potters see a finished photo and the glaze behind it. The assumptions list says a pot's record holds a finished photo and the glaze or combination together, and that failed pots are kept with an end state. The survey is read against those, and against the two objectives: "My pots' records are always safe" (which makes export and photo-loss stories the most relevant evidence) and "It earns its place in my studio" (which makes the in-studio habits below relevant).

Patterns across the apps:

- **The piece is the unit.** All of them hang everything off a piece record; stages run roughly formed, trimmed, dried, bisque, glazed, fired, finished. Stage lists are fixed in most; ClayLab lets only Pro users add, rename, reorder and delete stages, and PotteryNotes lets users rename fields rather than stages.
- **Glazes come as a shelf plus a per-piece pick.** The user keeps a list of their glazes (some preloaded with commercial names) and picks from it per piece. One ClayLab reviewer asked to be able to delete the preloaded names, which suggests unwanted preset lists can get in the way.
- **End states.** ClayLab has status values and an archive that its developer describes as a way to keep unsuccessful pieces without deleting the record; the Mud Room has Casualty and Recycled statuses and a casualty report with what happened, why and the lesson learned. PotteryNotes users asked for an archive so finished pieces leave the in-progress list.
- **Hand-building is under-served by throwing-shaped stage lists.** A 2019 Pottery Log reviewer says it suits potters who throw and asks for a hand-built option; ClayLab renamed "Thrown" to "Formed" in 2023.

### Web apps and offline-first apps (closest to this project's shape)

- **PotteryDave** is a web app that installs to the home screen ("Nothing to download"; it works in a phone or computer browser). Free plan: unlimited pieces, each stage dated with a cone for firings, predicted and measured shrinkage, one photo per piece within 500 MB, a shelf of clays, underglazes and glazes with every coat recorded per pot, and a one-tap datasheet card (clay, glazes, cone, sizes) with a caption for Instagram. Pieces are private until shared. It also offers a public gallery, following, comments and selling through the owner's own Stripe account (not surveyed). It needs an account. Its FAQ says records can be taken out with "Download everything" from the Account page; that page is behind the sign-in and the format is not described on the home page or in the blog post read. [Site](https://potterydave.com/), [blog post](https://potterydave.com/blog/what-s-new-and-what-s-to-come-ecd106db-c82f-4acf-a2b5-c7172f3472c1)
- **Claywell** is an iPhone app in beta (testers join through TestFlight), not a web app. Records are stored on the device with no account, the core tracking features work offline, and there is no sync between devices. It has a photo-first gallery found by stage, location, clay, glaze or collection; a notebook of notes and ideas that can link to pieces; surface layers recorded in order with materials, application method and coats; and a JSON backup that restores into another installation. Its guides page describes export in detail (see the export section). No hands-on or reviewer evidence was read. [Site](https://claywell.app/), [guides and FAQ](https://claywell.app/guides.html)

### Glaze databases and calculators

- **Glazy** (excerpt) is a free online recipe database and community. A recipe is materials in proportions plus structured metadata (picked from fixed categories, not free tags), photos, chemistry analysis and similarity search. It records recipes, not pots. Its own help says recipes export as a Recipe Card image that includes the recipe photograph, and as text files for Insight and GlazeChem; its changelog lists CSV export (2019) and YAML export (2023, expanded 2025). A community wishlist asks for PDF download of a recipe because the card is a JPEG. [Recipes guide](https://help.glazy.org/guide/recipes), [changelog](https://help.glazy.org/about/changelog), [wishlist](https://wiki.glazy.org/t/glazy-v2-wishlist/189.html)
- **Digitalfire Insight (desktop) and Insight-live** (excerpt) are glaze-chemistry tools. Insight-live is a cloud-hosted lab notebook for ceramics, keeping recipes, materials, firing schedules, projects, testing records and pictures under personal or group accounts. Pictures attach to recipes or test records, either uploaded or linked from an outside photo site. Desktop Insight keeps its recipes as XML files as an automatic backup, so its database can be rebuilt from them. Insight-live's import page lists CSV and XML sources. [Insight-live glossary](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/digitalfire+insight-live), [external image video notes](https://digitalfire.com/video/110), [desktop help](https://insight-live.com/insight/help/Desktop+Insight-344.html), [import and export help](https://insight-live.com/insight/help/Import+and+Export-332.html)
- **Pottery Tech's Glaze Log Book** (excerpt) is a small web tool: recipes with percentages, composition graphs, save, load, print and a Backup button that the page tells users to store in a cloud folder. File format not stated. [Page](https://potterytech.net/glaze-log-book/)

Nothing read in the databases ties a recipe to a photographed finished *pot*, or records a layered combination of two recipes as one thing. (Searched in excerpts of the Glazy help pages; no mention found. Not exhaustive.)

### Notebooks, spreadsheets, notes apps and templates

The Ceramic Arts Daily threads (excerpt) show how working potters actually keep records ([2021 thread on tracking pieces](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/23910-best-app-for-tracking-piecesnotes/), [2016 thread on glaze testing notes](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/13757-glaze-testing-charts-note-taking-s/)):

- **Paper notebook beside phone photos.** One potter draws the pot and notes inside glaze, outside glaze, any slip or wash, layer order, application method and thickness while glazing, and separately photographs each stage. Another photographs the fired tile and the notebook page so the two can be viewed side by side, and notes that a digital photo of a tile cannot break.
- **Marked test tiles.** A numbered mark on the back of each tile corresponds to an index-card or journal entry.
- **Wet-hands and dust.** Several potters keep devices out of the studio or say their hands are too dirty to use one mid-making, and write up afterwards; one adds that entering information in arrears is the usual app weakness.
- **General tools.** OneNote, Apple Notes with photos, and Airtable (one potter set up their own fields per piece and says entry is often late but useful) are used for per-piece records.
- **Printable and spreadsheet templates** are sold widely (Etsy listings include glaze test tile logs, glaze combination organisers, Google Sheets and Notion templates). One 2016 sheet design reserved a box for a photo of the finished piece. [Etsy glaze test listings](https://www.etsy.com/market/glaze_test)
- **Advice articles** stress a bound notebook, serial numbering and recording everything. [Pottery Making Illustrated](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/Recording-Glaze-Development)

## How each pairs a finished photo with its glaze

Six patterns appear.

1. **Both on the piece record.** PotteryNotes, Pottery Log, ClayLab, Dasti, PotteryDave, the Mud Room, Bisque-It and Claywell put photos and glaze fields on the same record, so the pairing is "this pot's photos" plus "this pot's glazes". It holds together as long as the record does. Photos and fields are separate lists inside the record, so nothing says which photo shows which glaze combination when a piece has several photos or several glazes. PotteryDave allows one photo per piece on the free plan, which makes the pairing unambiguous but thin. The Mud Room labels each photo with a stage (including Finished and Detail Shot), which says when a photo was taken but not which glaze layer it shows.
2. **Search from glaze to pots.** Pottery Log's reviewer calls search by glaze their favourite feature, and ClayLab reviewers describe finding work by glaze and clay body. This is how the "which glaze was that?" question gets answered in reverse, from a glaze to its pictures.
3. **Photo on the glaze, not the pot.** Glazy and Insight-live (both excerpt) attach pictures to recipes or test records; the Mud Room's glaze library holds up to three result photos per glaze, and its test tiles hold up to three photos with "layered over" and "layered under" fields. Good for test tiles and recipes; it does not say which finished pot used it.
4. **One composite image or sheet.** PotteryDave makes a datasheet card (clay, glazes, cone, sizes) from a piece for posting; its page does not say whether the card includes the piece's photo. ClayLab (from version 2.5) saves a finished piece as an image. Claywell's Showcase / Technical share creates one gallery-style image or PDF with the photos and details the potter selects. Glazy's Recipe Card (excerpt) puts the photo and recipe in one image, and Ceramispace (excerpt) exports glaze recipes to the photo library. These are the only pairings read that survive outside the app without extra work.
5. **A combination as a named entry.** The Mud Room's community glaze library entry has a combo name, clay body, cone, atmosphere, an ordered list of glaze layers, a description and up to two photos "to show the effect", and can be kept private ("My Private Combos"). Bisque-It lets a potter save and reuse glaze combinations (its page does not say how many fields or photos one holds). Claywell's example note "Remember this glaze combination" links to a piece, but a combination is a note there, not an entry of its own. Whether the Mud Room or Bisque-It links a combination entry to the pieces and finished photos that used it is not stated on the pages read.
6. **Paper plus photographs of paper.** The photo of the notebook page beside the tile photo, a photo box on a printed sheet, and numbers written on the back of tiles.

ClayLab and Dasti record ordered layers on each piece instead of a named combination. ClayNote's page (excerpt) says surface treatments can be annotated directly on photos; how that is stored and exported was not read.

## What potters dislike

Each item is one source's account, with the number of independent sources. The ClayLab and Pottery Log (Kenney) reviews and the PotteryNotes developer notice were read on the pages themselves; the PotteryNotes reviews, the Pottery Log (Kenney) Android review aggregator and the two forum threads were seen only as excerpts.

- **Layered glazes squeezed into one slot (several sources).** A 2022 ClayLab reviewer abandoned the app because only one glaze could be listed inside or outside, so they created a fake "multiple – see notes" entry, which defeated the point of a glaze list; they also wanted order of application. The developer added layers shortly after, and layer order and coats are now features (the latter Pro only). Another ClayLab reviewer in 2022 wanted several glazes per surface too; a forum potter notes combinations and order are exactly what gets forgotten.
- **Losing data (several sources).** PotteryNotes's own site reports that version 2.7.28, released on 1 June 2026 and pulled the next day, deleted users' photos when the app was opened; the developer calls it a major blunder and tells users to restore a recent iCloud device backup to get the photos back. Its backup pages (excerpt) tell users that backing up is their own responsibility, and recommend device backup, with iTunes File Sharing as a backup of the backup. A reviewer's account of the same loss (excerpt) says text notes survived. A 2021 forum poster says they had read reviews of PotteryNotes archives being erased by an update and found its cloud backup unreliable and hard to do. A forum potter in the same thread worries that proprietary apps have a backup problem and may go obsolete with no way to publish.
- **No way to get records out (one detailed source, echoed in the forum).** The same 2022 ClayLab reviewer said the missing export was the bigger flaw: if the app were discontinued or the phone changed they would lose what the journal is for, and asked for PDF or CSV. The developer replied that export, sync and backup were being looked at. Pro added import and export of all data in version 2.0 (June 2024), and the store listing now sells it as "Backup export" and "Backup import". A June 2024 reviewer still hoped for CSV, and the developer said it was on the roadmap for "the next month or two". The version history on the store page, which runs to 2.10.1, lists no CSV export.
- **Free-text names break search (one source).** A 2022 Pottery Log reviewer found glaze search case-sensitive, so "Celadon" and "celadon" give different results, especially for two-word names. The same reviewer wished friends at the studio could share glaze combination learnings, which is outside this version's scope.
- **Search or filter that forgets or cannot reach glaze (several sources).** A 2019 PotteryNotes reviewer could not search glaze or surface treatments; a 2023 Pottery Log reviewer lost the search when going back from a result.
- **Stage lists that do not fit the potter's way (several sources).** No stage between bisqued and glazed (Pottery Log, 2023); no hand-building option (Pottery Log, 2019); PotteryNotes users wanting sections by stage; ClayLab users wanting to drop size and weight and add price or firing schedule (a 2022 reviewer; the app later added custom stages and surface areas, partly on Pro).
- **In-progress list clutter (one source).** PotteryNotes users could not archive completed pieces to another list.
- **Cross-device sharing (one source).** A 2018 PotteryNotes reviewer had to transfer each page separately from phone to tablet.
- **Photo handling (one source).** A Pottery Log reviewer could zoom but not choose which area a photo displayed.
- **Studio conditions (forum, several posters).** Dust, wet hands and clay on electronics push potters to paper and to writing up later. Late entry is the cost.
- **Paper's weakness (forum).** A potter likes paper but says it cannot hold the photo that shows the outcome, which is what they need to reproduce or tweak a result.

## What this means for the hops that build on it

These are findings relevant to each hop, not choices.

- **Pot records and stages.** Ideas that recur and have user demand: per-piece stages with dates; an end state (failed, gifted, sold) and an archive instead of delete; potter-editable stages (ClayLab gates this behind Pro; PotteryNotes allows field renaming); a stage between bisque and glaze; forming methods that include hand-building; separate firing records. Ideas that drew complaints: fixed stage lists, preloaded glaze names that cannot be removed, size and weight fields that cannot be hidden, and a single list mixing in-progress and finished pieces.
- **Finished photo and glaze.** The pairing that works is one record that holds the finished photo with an *ordered list* of glazes, searchable from glaze to pots. Named combination entries exist (the Mud Room, Bisque-It) but how they tie back to finished-pot photos is not stated. Evidence of trouble: one-glaze-per-surface, unrecorded order, free-text names that split search, and no record of which photo shows which combination when a piece has several. A composite image or sheet that carries the photo and glaze text is the only pairing that survives outside the app (ClayLab, Claywell, Glazy, and PotteryDave's card if it carries the photo).
- **Full export.** The strongest unmet need in the reviews is getting everything out. Photo loss and app abandonment are the fears named. Formats documented as open: PDF, CSV, JSON, YAML, images. Claywell (offline-first, no account) is the nearest to this project's shape and documents a split worth knowing about: a JSON file that restores everything including photos, and a CSV that is readable but cannot restore and carries only a photo count. The Mud Room's CSVs are per category. No tool read documents a human-readable view alongside the restorable file, and the formats of ClayLab's, PotteryDave's and Bisque-It's exports are not named.
